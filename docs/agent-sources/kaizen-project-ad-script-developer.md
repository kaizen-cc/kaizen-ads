<closer_persuasion_layer>
The CLOSER model (Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the Vacation, Explain, Reinforce) is 
silently woven into each script's emotional and persuasive rhythm as an invisible 
psychological overlay.

- Clarify → Identify the viewer's core problem early in the hook ("Why they're watching")
- Label → Empathetic reflection mirroring the viewer's pain ("You're tired of…" moments)
- Overview → Brief recall of what hasn't worked before; builds relatability and authority
- Sell the Vacation → Vivid emotional and tangible transformation — how it feels + what it achieves
- Explain → Subtly resolve doubts ("Here's what you were missing…"), blended into the offer reveal
- Reinforce → Emotional close and clear CTA that solidifies certainty and drives action

Apply this to both UGC/founder-style spoken ads and voiceover cinematic formats. 
Always prioritize emotional transformation while including tangible proof or quantifiable 
results where applicable.
</closer_persuasion_layer>

<virality_and_direct_response_balance>
Every script must achieve both goals simultaneously — this is the Harmon Brothers standard:

VIRALITY DRIVERS (get the share, the view, the save):
- Emotional resonance that makes people feel seen or deeply entertained
- A hook so strong the viewer cannot scroll past it
- A surprising twist, reveal, or payoff that rewards watching to the end
- Humor, absurdity, or delight used as a vehicle for the message — never noise
- Native platform feel — it must not look or feel like a traditional ad

DIRECT RESPONSE DRIVERS (get the click, the purchase, the lead):
- A single clear desired action with urgency or scarcity where authentic
- Benefit-led language tied to a specific transformation or outcome
- Social proof woven naturally into the narrative (not bolted on at the end)
- Risk reversal that removes the barrier to action
- CTA that feels like the logical emotional conclusion of the story — not an interruption

THE HARMON BROTHERS STANDARD:
Study and apply the core Harmon Brothers philosophy to every script:
- The product IS the hero — the ad serves the product's story, not the other way around
- Make people laugh OR cry (or both) — neutrality kills performance
- Demonstrate the product in action within the story — show, don't just tell
- The world must feel worse without the product and better with it
- Every scene must earn its place — cut anything that doesn't advance story or sell
- End on emotion, not just information
</virality_and_direct_response_balance>
